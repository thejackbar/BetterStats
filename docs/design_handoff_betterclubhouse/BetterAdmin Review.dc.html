Turn 1 · Design review · BetterAdmin umbrella

Four sub-modules, four products

BetterFees, BetterComms and BetterMerch were each built against ModuleLayout, but they disagree on where the page title lives, what a button looks like, and what colour text sits on the accent. BetterClubManager's redesign left ModuleLayout entirely and built its own shell — which is why it looks better and also why it lost the platform chrome. Below: the audit, a name, one information architecture, the screens as they are today, and the glow up.

1a Cohesion audit · what each module does & where it diverges

What each sub-module actually does

BetterFees7 TOOLS · AMBER

A per-season subscription ledger. Members carry a tier and a membership type; match-day fees accrue from appearances via SYNC MATCH DAYS. Payments are logged manually, bulk, or imported from a bank CSV; Square and Xero each get their own page. Reports and a rate card round it out.

OWNS  fee_members · tiers · payments · seasons rollover
BetterComms6 TOOLS · AMBER

Bulk email. A campaign has a subject, body, UTM tags and an audience; audiences come from three different concepts — Contacts (its own person list), Lists (static) and Segments (saved rule filters). Templates and provider settings sit alongside. A context bar switches between the club and the prospect directory.

OWNS  contacts · segments · lists · templates · provider
BetterMerch6-7 TOOLS · AMBER

Stock and equipment. Products carry variants with cost/retail, reorder points and expiry; movements are typed (received, sold, issued, used, write-off, stocktake, adjustment). Equipment assets have condition, service and replacement dates. An optional storefront adds orders. Square again, reports again.

OWNS  products · variants · movements · assets · orders
BetterClubManager8 SCREENS · INDIGO

The back office. Today aggregates what needs you; Directory is one record per person with roles, qualifications, volunteer hours, committee terms and families; Roster fills shifts against areas; plus Committee, Club Diary, Facilities, Events and an Areas & Roles setup screen.

OWNS  people spine · roles · quals · hours · diary · areas

Note the shape of it: three of the four are ledgers of things (money, emails, stock) and the fourth is the club itself. ClubManager isn't a peer of the other three — it's the substrate they all stand on. That is the single most important finding in this review, and it's what the naming and the IA below both follow from.

THE REAL PROBLEM

The redesign that fixed the look also forked the shell. ClubManager now has the good screen header and grouped nav, but no module switcher, no bookmarking, no user menu — and a hard-coded indigo. The fix is not to restyle three modules to match a fork; it is to promote ClubManager's shell into the shared one, then move all four onto it.

KEEP FROM CLUBMANAGER

The sticky screen header with a mono sub-caption. Grouped nav headings. The 300px list + detail pane. Stat cards with accent numerals and mono labels. Pill filters instead of checkboxes. Sentence-case buttons. Everything at 8px radius.

KEEP FROM THE OTHERS

Merch's KPI row and its three-up alert cards — that pattern is already the right one and should be the house dashboard block. Fees' dense mono-headed tables, which are genuinely good for reconciliation. Comms' rule builder, which is the best editor in the four.

1b Functional overlap · duplication · what to collapse

Ordered by how much it costs you. The first one is the expensive one.

01
Three separate person lists, and the same CSV importer written twice

Fees keeps per-season member rows with a tier. ClubManager's Directory is explicitly built on the same table as its person spine, and ships its own add-person modal and CSV importer whose columns are character-for-character identical to Fees' — name, email, mobile, category, roles. Comms then keeps a third list, Contacts, with a source field that admits the problem out loud: a contact can be "a player", "a fee member", "imported", or "added manually".

DO THIS

One Directory is the club's only person list. Fees stops owning people and owns a fee account that hangs off a directory person. Comms Contacts is deleted as a concept — an audience is a query over the Directory. One importer, one add-person modal, one "who is this?" answer.

02
A member can owe money in two ledgers that never meet

Fees tracks payable / paid / outstanding per member and stamps them OWES or FINANCIAL. Merch separately has an issued movement kind that carries money and a headline KPI called "Owed by members". So a player who owes subs and a training shirt is financial in one screen and owing in another, and nobody can answer "what does this family owe the club?"

DO THIS

One account per person, with subs, match fees and issued kit as line items on it. Merch's "issue" action posts a charge to that account instead of a private balance. Family grouping already exists in the Directory, so a household balance falls out for free — and "financial" becomes a single, trustworthy word.

03
Square is connected twice; reports and settings are scattered four ways

There is a Square page under Fees and a Square page under Merch — two integration surfaces for one merchant account. Reports exist under Fees, under Merch, and in the main admin. Configuration is spread across Fees' membership types, Comms' provider settings, ClubManager's Areas & Roles, and the global admin settings, so "where do I change that?" has no reliable answer.

DO THIS

One Integrations page listing Square, Xero and the email provider as connections with a status each. One Reports screen with a source selector, since every report already shares the same season and person dimensions. Setup collapses to two screens: Areas & roles, and Integrations.

04
"Event" means four different things — and you were right to flag it

In ClubManager an event is a club activity on a date with registration that members attend. In Comms it is telemetry: segment rules ask whether someone was emailed, opened, clicked, visited a page. BetterCRM has a third events view for pipeline activity, and the social post editor has an event post editor. Same word, four meanings, one umbrella.

DO THIS

Reserve Event for the thing a member can attend — nothing else gets the word in the UI. Comms telemetry is renamed Activity in rule labels ("has opened an email", "has clicked a link"), which reads better anyway. Then a club event can be an audience: "registered for the presentation night" becomes a legal rule, which is the integration you actually want.

05
Contacts, Lists and Segments are three answers to one question

A club officer wanting to email the under-15 parents has to work out whether that's a list, a segment, or a filter on contacts. Meanwhile the Directory already ships exactly the right filter vocabulary — Everyone, Players, Volunteers, Committee, Parents, Third parties, plus "quals to renew" — and it can't be emailed from.

DO THIS

One concept: Audience. Every audience is a saved query over the Directory; a hand-picked list is just an audience whose rule is "these people". Put Email these 34 people as a button on the Directory itself, so filtering and sending are one motion instead of two screens apart.

06
Expiry-and-renewal is implemented twice with the same three tones

ClubManager grades qualifications as current / expires soon / expired and lets an expiry block rostering. Merch grades stock as fine / expiring / expired and equipment as due / overdue for service. Two implementations, identical semantics, identical green-amber-red.

DO THIS

One renewal primitive — a thing, a date, a lead time, an owner — used by qualifications, stock expiry, equipment service, insurance and affiliations. Every one of them then feeds Today without new code, which is where they belong.

07
Your own sales telemetry is shipped inside a club-facing module

The Comms segment builder carries a second field set for the prospect directory: is-trialing, requested-a-trial, had-a-demo, customer status, engagement score, which marketing pages a club visited. That is BetterCRM's job, and it sits one dropdown away from a volunteer treasurer's screen.

DO THIS

Move the prospect field set behind BetterCRM and drop the context bar from the club build entirely. Same engine, two mounts — clubs get club fields, you get prospect fields.

08
Two role vocabularies, and only one of them is visible

Club roles (Canteen Manager, First Aid Officer) live in the Areas & Roles catalogue and are what rostering and qualifications gate on. Cricket roles (BAT, BWL, ALL, WK) come from the player record and are what Comms segments filter on. A committee role is a third thing again, held as a position with a term.

DO THIS

Keep them separate but label them honestly everywhere: club role, committee position, playing role. The Directory already shows all three on one record — that record is the reconciliation, and nothing else needs to merge.

The six moments that make it one product

Cohesion isn't paint. These are the joins that make a club officer feel they're in one tool — each one is only possible once the consolidations above are done, which is the argument for doing them.

→

34 owe subs on Today → opens the account list already filtered → Email these 34 drops you into a compose with the audience attached and a reminder template chosen.

→

Issue a playing shirt in Stock → pick the person from the Directory → the charge lands on their account, and their balance updates in the same second.

→

Open registration on an event → it takes payments through the one Square connection and sends its confirmation through the one email provider. No second setup.

→

Two canteen shifts unfilled on Saturday → email everyone with the Canteen role who isn't already rostered, from the roster gap itself.

→

One person record carries account balance, roles, quals, hours, committee terms, family and kit issued. Open it from anywhere in the module and it's the same drawer.

→

Today is the module's front door, aggregating money, stock, renewals, roster gaps and the diary — Merch's alert cards and ClubManager's attention rows are already 80% of this.

1c Naming

Keeping the Better prefix. The name has to cover subs, email, stock and the committee without sounding like enterprise software — so it should name a place, not a function. Nobody says "I'll do the club administration"; they say "I'm heading down to the club".

RECOMMENDED
BetterClubhouse

The clubhouse is the one building where all four things physically happen: subs get paid at the bar, the noticeboard carries the announcements, the bar and canteen stock sits in the storeroom, and the committee meets upstairs. It's warm, spoken, entirely non-corporate, and it survives you adding a seventh tool because a clubhouse can hold anything.

COVERS ALL FOUR LITERALLY 3 SYLLABLES SPORT-NEUTRAL
ALSO STRONG
BetterPavilion

Cricket-native and affectionate — the pavilion is the club's admin building. Slightly more beautiful than Clubhouse and slightly less obvious; the cost is that it doesn't travel to other sports as cleanly, if that ever matters.

BetterBackroom

Names the work honestly and flatters the people doing it — the backroom is where the club is actually run. Punchy and un-corporate. The risk is that it sounds like something hidden, when half of this module is member-facing.

BetterKeeper

The wicketkeeper is the one who takes everything, and a club "keeps" its books, its stock and its records. The best pun on the list. Docked a place because "keeper" reads as a single job title, and this module is many.

CONSIDERED AND REJECTED
BetterClubManager — your own instinct is right to move off it. It's three words long, it names a person's job title rather than the club, and it's already taken by one of the four sub-modules, so keeping it means the umbrella and a section share a name.
BetterAdmin — accurate and completely inert. "Admin" is the word volunteers use for the part of the job they resent.
BetterHQ / BetterOps — short and snappy, but both are startup-office words in a module used by a treasurer with a spiral notebook.
BetterLedger, BetterKitbag, BetterTuckshop — each names one quarter of the module and mis-sells the other three.
WHAT HAPPENS TO THE OLD NAMES

They become section headings inside the sidebar and stop being brands: Money, Stock, Comms, People, Club. One lockup, one accent, one switcher entry — four fewer products for a club to be sold, learn and pay for separately.

1d Unified information architecture

One module, one sidebar, six groups

Four flat sidebars of 6–7 items each becomes one grouped sidebar — ClubManager's nav shape, extended. Different officers use different groups, so the sidebar collapses groups the current user has no capability for; a treasurer sees Today, Money and People and nothing else, which is fewer items than BetterFees shows them now.

MOBILE — YOU ASKED FOR REAL SUPPORT

Right now the shared layout hides the sidebar under a hamburger below 768px while the ClubManager redesign switches at 1280px and drops its detail pane — two different breakpoints and two different drawers. How I'd sequence it

1 · SHELL

Promote the ClubManager screen header, grouped nav and card scale into the shared layout, and give it back the switcher and account in the sidebar footer. Every module gets the glow up in one change, with no screen rewritten.

2 · LANGUAGE

One button component, one input, one pill, one stat card, one table head — deleting the four local copies. Mono caps stop being buttons and go back to being labels. This is where most of the visual incoherence dies.

3 · MERGE THE NAV

One sidebar, six groups, capability-gated. Rename to BetterClubhouse, one lockup, one amber accent, one switcher entry. Contacts / Lists / Segments collapse into Audiences; the two Square pages into Integrations.

4 · JOIN THE DATA

The Directory becomes the only person list; Fees and Merch balances become one account. This is the real work and the real payoff — every cross-module moment in 1b becomes possible, and Today can finally speak for the whole club.
